# Content formula

Six-beat scaffold for post-length content: LinkedIn essays, blog articles, talk arcs, long-form
pieces, podcast scripts. Not for short Slack messages, replies, status updates.

## The beats

1. Hook / Challenge. Where readers lack the answer, open with the friction: a small observation (preferred), a
   specific moment, or a quick framing of something common but unnamed. No stat-shaped boasts, no
   mythic framing; research that backs the experience belongs mid-post as validation, never in the
   hook. On LinkedIn the hook is the first line on its own, under 100 characters. *"Sprint 1 with
   AI agents looks worse than the sprint before. Most teams quit there."*
2. Personal Story / Experience. Named people, real moments, public numbers, sourced in the closing `(Source: ...)` line. The story
   is the credential; skip "in our 20 years..." preamble. *"Sprint 1-2 are exhausting. Sprint 3-4
   you break even. Sprint 5-6 you're faster."*
3. Deeper Question. The reframe, where peer language replaces vendor language. *"The dip is the
   cost of the foundation the team is building."*
4. Practical Insight. The thing the reader can do Monday, concrete and tied to the story. *"In the
   first two weeks, track one number: how much the team has encoded into CLAUDE.md and shared
   agents."*
5. Human Impact. The translate-up to engineering culture, team health, programming joy. *"By
   sprint 6, standup is about what to build next."*
6. Call to Adventure. Forward motion, not summary: on LinkedIn a closing statement the reader can
   act on, a question only where it is a real one; a clear next step for blog and long-form; a
   Monday-morning first move for a talk.

In an article, the thesis and the rule the reader should take away sit in the first quarter: most
readers leave at 50 to 60% of the page (Chartbeat, 2013).

A worked example that runs through an article sits in labelled, visibly bounded passages, and every
general statement stays outside them.

A headline names the problem, not the win: in news headlines each negative word raised the
click-through rate by 2.3% (Robertson et al., Nature Human Behaviour, 2023).

A long piece is published on the blog and teased with a native text post: the LinkedIn Article
format reached a median of 596 against 921 for text posts (AuthoredUp, 2026, 372,000 posts).

## Pattern: research-backed posts

When citing external research, the source name leads only where the source itself is the news.

1. Open with h&w's lived experience: what we observe in practice, before any data. "We've seen it
   enough times that we can predict it before the team can."
2. Bring in the data as validation mid-post: "That turns out to be a measurable pattern."
3. Cite figures in the body with no source name inline, just the numbers.
4. One `(Source: Name, date)` line after the close.

A piece that ends in a sources list names the source in the sentence carrying the figure instead.

State what a study measured, then mark the conclusion you draw from it as yours.

Anti-pattern: "[Source] just put a number on why."
