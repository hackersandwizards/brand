## Method

- Layouts, from real screenshots only (os `docs/agent-work/2026-10-31-channel-banners-live/`). App:
  the left pane of `verlauf-app-profile-vorher.jpg`, the iPhone app at about 2x, the banner 790 x
  196 px; `app-2253.jpg` confirms the phone's width. The screenshot is the owner's view: the two
  edit pencils were painted out. Laptop: the right half of `live-linkedin-benedikt.jpg`, the
  desktop site logged out, the banner 718 x 180 px at the screenshot's scale. Each candidate banner
  went into the measured box and the real photo, with its ring, back over it (app: centre x 122, y
  281, radius 91.5 px; laptop: centre x 104, y 192, radius 65 px). The left half of
  `live-linkedin-benedikt.jpg` was never rendered or scored.
- Banners from `applications/channel-banners/render.py` (`profile-1`, `x-profile`,
  `sessionize-profile`), loaded by a scratch script. The descriptor "Training and mentoring in
  agentic engineering" / "Training und Mentoring in Agentic Engineering" sits at 0.62 of the claim
  size, no accent colour. A sentence too long for one row breaks where the two rows come out most
  even. Closing slide: the slides catalog story `Types/closing-slide/contact`, claim swapped in
  Chrome for Testing, headline "Danke." on the German slide. Link preview: the website's
  `scripts/og/template.html` from `origin/main`, filled as `generate.mjs` fills the home card.
- Cold reads of the banner alone, the photo over it: CTO, VP of Engineering, engineering lead, HR
  at a logistics company; Claude (`claude -p --model opus`) and Codex (`gpt-6.1-sol`, high); two
  runs, the answer options reversed in run 2. Each persona and model read one run in the app and
  one on the laptop, the order alternating by persona. The team question ("unclear" offered) and the
  free-text question each ran on their own. 16 readers per line, language and question: 640 runs.
- Classifier: Claude runs that saw only the 320 readings, never a line, marking people, alone,
  company and the kind of company; core is people and company. Three passes, shuffled; a reading
  counts by the majority.
- Panel: round 9's five personas (CTO, VP of Engineering, conference organiser, senior engineer
  looking for a job, brand strategist), both models, two runs. Each run saw one language: all 12
  sheets, in its own shuffled order under neutral ids. Each sheet: the LinkedIn app and laptop
  views side by side, the X header, the Sessionize cover, the closing slide, the link preview.
  Round 9's nine criteria (distinctive, clear, emotional, credible, calm, language, core, people,
  together), then banners, slide, link preview and overall, 0 to 100. 40 runs.
- Set: f6, m3, s4, u1 and s4p, plus u1p, which ties s4 on the panel; the sixth set cost no
  extra run. Each set: its English and German sheets. Beside them, as text: the home H1 with
  German "zusammenspielen", the lead, the tuning desk, the 24 decided page headlines (website item
  `work-ship-decided-page-headlines` from `origin/main`, blog-r2) and four profile texts, each
  opening with the claim line: the LinkedIn About from the screenshot, the practitioners short bio
  in both languages (`origin/main`), and the Sessionize bio, read live today. Personas: CTO, VP of
  Engineering, brand strategist, bilingual copy editor. Both models, two runs: 16 runs.
- Noise: the gap between the means of run 1 and run 2. A lead counts only outside the noise, in
  both runs and with both models; otherwise it is a tie.
- 722 runs: 640 cold reads, 24 classifier, 40 panel, 16 set, 2 command checks.

## Decision

Recommendation: u1. It meets (a), (b), (c), (d) and (e), and leads the set outside the noise with
both models. It misses (f): its German scores 4.4 below its English, and reviewers find "gemeinsam
liefern" less natural than "ship together" (language 7.7 against 8.8). s4 and s4p meet the same
requirements and miss the same one, so the tie goes to the set: u1 leads s4p there by 7.6, outside
the noise with both models, and s4 by 1.9 with Claude.

- s4 is the closest alternative. It ties u1 on the panel and in the set (Claude has u1 ahead),
  but ties f6 in the set rather than beating it, and its "team" piles onto the site's. It misses
  (f) by less (2.2).
- s4p leads the panel, with Claude only. In the set Claude puts it 8.8 behind f6, and both models
  put it behind s4 and u1: the second sentence makes two lengths of one claim and repeats the bios.
- m3 has the strongest banners and German, and ties u1 in the set. It misses (d) in every round
  (together 5.2).
- f6 misses (a): 13 of 32 cold readers restate the core thought.

Choosing u1 changes `verbal.json`'s claim to one line with no long form; the profile banners break
it over two rows. That is a release; `home-claim-line` keeps "zusammenspielen".
