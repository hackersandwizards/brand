The pair r1 with hb scores 70.8 on the reader's path. That is 4.8 below r1 with the headline it replaces and 2.2 above the earlier claim tpe-tune with hb. Almost all of the cost comes from one model: Claude scores the pair 9.4 lower than r1 with the old headline, Codex 0.1 lower. Every reviewer but one writes that banner and H1 say the same thing.

### What reviewers write about the two lines saying the same thing

A blind classifier (Claude, three shuffled passes, majority) read every remark of the 384 reviews, what keeps the pair below the maximum and the question it leaves open, without the pair it belongs to. Seven planted remarks came back as planted: two that name the repetition, two that do not, one fragment, one "already" twice and one "already" in another sense.

- **The repetition:** 63 of 64 reviews of r1+hb name it, with both models and in both languages. With the old headline r1 draws it in 43 of 64 (Claude 28 of 32, Codex 15 of 32), tpe with hb in 13 of 64, tpe with the old headline in 7 of 64. So r1 alone already repeats the headline's "releases" and "in tune" for most reviewers, and hb adds the same opening.
- **In their words:** "Claim and H1 are near-identical, so neither does its own job; the H1 ends on a fragment." (VP of Engineering, Claude). "Claim and H1 repeat the same promise, and the H1 ends on a sentence fragment." (CTO, Claude). "Banner und H1 wiederholen viel; eingespielt bleibt vage, und Sobald verspricht einen zu unmittelbaren Zusammenhang." (copy editor, Codex). "The benefit is immediate, but banner and H1 repeat closely; the final fragment makes alignment sound like a prerequisite." (brand strategist, Codex, who scores the pair 84).
- **The question it leaves open:** "What does the H1 add beyond what the banner already said?" and, most often, what getting in tune takes: "What concretely do you change so all roles are in tune?".
- **The fragment:** 48 of 64 reviews of r1+hb and 47 of 64 of tpe+hb name the headline's second sentence, "Once all roles are in tune." / "Sobald alle Rollen zusammenspielen.", as a fragment: for r1+hb 30 of 32 English reviews and 18 of 32 German ones, Claude in 30 of 32 and Codex in 18 of 32. It belongs to hb with either claim.
- **"Already" twice:** no review names it. The home page in every sheet shows the subline with "already use" under hb's "already have". The remark came up in the hero rounds of 10.11., where reviewers judged the first screen alone.

### What this tells the surfaces

These are my readings of the scores, for the briefs below. None changes a line.

- A surface that shows both lines one after the other, the profile banner and then the home page, or the H1 and the footer of the same page, has to make the copy between them add something: what getting in tune takes and what h&w does for it. That is the question nearly every reviewer leaves with.
- The website's home subline is the place for it. It needs a new sentence in any case, since "already" stands in the headline now.
- A profile text under the banner should not open on "faster releases with agents" a second time. The round of 08.11. found the same for the claim before: pasting the claim under a banner that says it scored lowest.
- The headline's second sentence reads as a fragment to most reviewers in English. The website's hero break decides how hard that lands; a website run should score the break with the line rendered.
