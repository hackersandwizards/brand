### What reviewers write about arriving with the same thought

A blind classifier (Claude, three shuffled passes, majority) read what each of the 256 case reviews writes about the relation to the memory, what works and what keeps it below the maximum, without the pair it belongs to. It marks a review as help where it names the match between memory and screen as something that works, and as cost where it names the match as what holds the screen back. Nine planted remarks came back as planted: three help, three cost, one both and two neither. 246 of 265 were unanimous.

- **r1+hb is the one pair where the cost is common:** 15 of 32 reviews, against 8 for r1+dh, 3 for u1+dh and none for the five other pairs. Help is named in 20 of 32, the most of any pair; r1+dh has 17, e5x+hb and x4+dh 16.

### Limits of this round

- The reviewers are model personas, and the memory is a takeaway a model wrote minutes earlier. No days pass, and nobody forgets.
- The round measures likeness of thought only. Whether a reader who still has the banner's words in mind hears them again in the H1 is what the round of 10.11. measured.
- The first screen alone was shown. The home page's footer carries the claim and its link preview the headline, so for the two lines on one page the round of 10.11. stays the measurement.
- The earlier visit is the LinkedIn profile with the descriptor. No other banner was the way in.
- A word search for the headline's second sentence read as a fragment (fragment, abrupt, incomplete and their German forms) finds it in 2 of 32 reviews of r1+hb, where the classifier of 10.11. found it in 48 of 64. The search does hit the remarks quoted from that round, but this prompt has no criterion on language or on the headline's job, so the two counts do not compare and the finding of 10.11. stands.
- Mixed-up images. Each prompt holds the two first screens four times each under eight file names. One Claude review of the main arm (CTO, run 1, English) describes the other headline in four of its eight cases. The rule for it was written before the second half's outputs were read: a review whose hb case scores lower on gain first than one of its dh cases, and whose remarks describe the other headline, is run once more. The rerun still swaps two cases. The tables hold the rerun; with the first run in its place no pair moves by more than 0.3. In all, 4 of 256 cases of the main arm carry the gain score of the other headline, all in Claude's English reviews. In the control one review (CTO, Claude, run 2, English) swaps e5x+dh and x4+hb, and its rerun swaps the same two; the control's table holds the rerun, and the first run moves no pair by more than 0.6. Prompt, key and image files agree for every case, checked by file hash. A swap moves an hb pair and a dh pair towards each other, so where it changes anything it shrinks hb's lead over dh. Why the reviewer swaps them is not established.
- The rule file counts stage 2 as 64 runs. Four personas, two models, two runs and two languages are 32, each scoring eight pairs.
