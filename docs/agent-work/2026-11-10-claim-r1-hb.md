### What reviewers write about the two lines saying the same thing

A blind classifier (Claude, three shuffled passes, majority) read every remark of the 384 reviews, what keeps the pair below the maximum and the question it leaves open, without the pair it belongs to. Seven planted remarks came back as planted: two that name the repetition, two that do not, one fragment, one "already" twice and one "already" in another sense.

- **In their words:** "Claim and H1 are near-identical, so neither does its own job; the H1 ends on a fragment." (VP of Engineering, Claude). ### What this tells the surfaces

These are my readings of the scores, for the briefs below. None changes a line.

- A surface that shows both lines one after the other, the profile banner and then the home page, or the H1 and the footer of the same page, has to make the copy between them add something: what getting in tune takes and what h&w does for it. That is the question nearly every reviewer leaves with.
- The website's home subline is the place for it. It needs a new sentence in any case, since "already" stands in the headline now.
- A profile text under the banner should not open on "faster releases with agents" a second time. The round of 08.11. found the same for the claim before: pasting the claim under a banner that says it scored lowest.
- The headline's second sentence reads as a fragment to most reviewers in English. The website's hero break decides how hard that lands; a website run should score the break with the line rendered.
