# A new claim for everywhere, 06.11.2026

The decision is the item `new-claim`. This report keeps the method, the sources and every
candidate's scores, newest round first.

## The question

Benedikt, 06.11., answering which home headline to take (relayed by Ada): "irgendwas mit version 4
bitte wir brauchen da einen komplett neuen claim überall". Version 4 is option d of the website item
`home-headline`. d won as the home H1, a full sentence. A claim also stands in the website footer,
on one-pagers, on the deck closing slide, in the press boilerplate, on LinkedIn banners and in
`verbal.json`. So the search was for a claim in d's direction: an outcome at the customer, speed at
a pace that holds, energy and calm together, short enough to stand without a lead sentence. d and a
shortened d ran as candidates.

## What claim practice says

- A B2B tagline earns its place by doing one job and by its relevance to the buyer's own pressure.
  A tagline built on table stakes rather than what the firm does uniquely is "merely serviceable"
  (DeSantis Breindel, "B2B Taglines: The Hardest Working Words in Branding", fetched 06.11.).
- In Germany, English claims are understood less than their senders assume: 64 percent of 1,204
  respondents said they do not understand English claims correctly, and only 48 percent said they
  know what German claims mean (YouGov Deutschland, 2015 fieldwork, published 25.02.2016,
  fetched 06.11.). The finding is ten years old and about consumer claims. It still argues for a
  German claim of its own rather than the English one on German pages.
- The searches turned up no controlled study of tagline length or of outcome against mood framing
  for B2B services. Practitioner advice favours short lines and one outcome. The scores below test
  that directly on h&w's own surfaces.

## Candidates

Rounds 1 to 3 searched in d's direction: an outcome at the customer, speed at a pace that holds,
energy and calm together, short enough to stand without a lead sentence. Round 1 scored 16 pairs:
ten drafts of this run, three from an independent draft (a Claude model that saw the offer, the
buyer and the rules, but neither the current claim nor d) and three anchors. Round 2 refined the
top five. Round 3 tested the top two, a German word-order fix and the current claim without
"unstoppable".

Round 4 kept a's strengths, energy and calm and a short, memorable line, and worked on a's measured
gaps: "calm" read as a precondition, distinctive at about 6, the German sentence-final "früher" read
as "formerly", and no word of training. It scored nine new drafts and three of six pairs from a new
independent draft, a fresh Claude model told the offer, the buyer, the energy-and-calm direction
and the rules, with none of the candidates. a, b and the current claim ran as unmarked controls. A
second pass refined the top five. The `voice` skill's rules applied to every draft: no hype, no
contrast frame, no triplet, ihr-form German that reads spoken.

Round 5 put flow, the claim's word, and the home hero visual's tune idea into n8's two-part shape.
Round 6 tested four lines Benedikt proposed after round 5, variations on each of their ideas and new
drafts in the same territory: flow as the outcome, and what it needs (practice, the right setup,
teams in tune, the organization around them).

## Method

- Medium and size, rounds 1 to 4: the website home hero at 1440 x 900 with the live page's lead
  sentence under the H1 and the hero visual frozen in one state (reduced motion), the deck closing
  slide at 1920 x 1080 from the slides catalog, and from round 3 on the one-pager header (in round 4
  the documents catalog's one-pager with the AE-LEAD title and intro). Each was rendered headless
  from the live page with the candidate in place of the claim, then stacked into one image per pair
  and language, 631 x 1000 in round 4. The closing slide's highlight word was off for every
  candidate, the current claim included; in round 4 the German slide reads "Danke.".
- The home H1 test: the first screen at 1440 x 900 and at 375 x 812 on a phone, each 1000 px on its
  long side, the lead sentence unchanged.
- Round 5: the claim test stacks the closing slide and the AE-LEAD one-pager header only,
  1000 x 1000. The home H1 test shows the hero visual running: the tuning desk held lined up, "in flow",
  with its waveform animating (no reduced motion), the 1440 px first screen at 1000 x 625 and the
  375 px phone from the top down to the desk, 322 x 1000, with the page's own language set in the
  browser.
- Round 6: the same two tests and renders as round 5. The claim test keeps one image per language;
  the home H1 test joins the two languages, so that 28 candidates stay under 100 images per
  reviewer: the 1440 px first screens, English over German, 790 x 1000, and the 375 px phones,
  English beside German, about 610 x 1000. The hero visual was
  held lined up by setting each slider to its own target, which stops the automatic swing and keeps
  the waveform running.
- Personas: a CTO of a Mittelstand manufacturer under board pressure, a VP of Engineering at a
  scale-up, a Head of Engineering worried about quality and morale, a brand strategist for DACH B2B
  tech services, and a senior developer after a two-day training.
- Criteria, fixed before the first score: clear, credible, distinctive, energy and calm, memorable,
  language (each 0 to 10), and an overall score from 0 to 100. The home H1 and page tests dropped
  memorable; the page test added fit, and round 5's home H1 test fit with the hero visual.
- Reviewers: Claude (`claude -p --model opus`) and Codex (`gpt-6.1-sol`, high). Each saw its own
  shuffled order under neutral labels, with nothing marking a line as current or naming its author.
- Noise is the mean difference between two runs of the same reviewer, and on the panel mean, the
  number the tables compare. Rounds 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 ran twice per reviewer; round 2 once.
- The persona texts of rounds 5 and 6 are round 3's: the same five roles, worded slightly differently from
  round 4's.
- The controls move with the field: a scored 74.2 (EN) as the claim in round 3 and 62.5 in round
  4's second pass, beside stronger lines. The current claim stayed between 24.5 and 28.3 in English
  in every round. Compare lines within one table.

Surfaces rendered: the home hero, the closing slide and the one-pager header (the claim test of rounds 5
and 6: the slide and the one-pager only). Not rendered: the website footer, LinkedIn banners, the mail
signature and the press boilerplate. The reviewers were told that the line stands in each of these
places.
