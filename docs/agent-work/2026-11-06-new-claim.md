# A new claim for everywhere, 06.11.2026

The decision is the item `new-claim`. This report keeps the method, the sources and every
candidate's scores.

## The question

Benedikt, 06.11., answering which home headline to take (relayed by Ada): "irgendwas mit version 4
bitte wir brauchen da einen komplett neuen claim überall". Version 4 is option d of the website item
`home-headline`. d won as the home H1, a full sentence. A claim also stands in the website footer,
on one-pagers, on the deck closing slide, in the press boilerplate, on LinkedIn banners and in
`verbal.json`. So the search was for a claim in d's direction: an outcome at the customer, speed at
a pace that holds, energy and calm together, short enough to stand without a lead sentence. d and a
shortened d ran as candidates.

## What claim practice says

- A B2B tagline earns its place by doing one job and by its relevance to the buyer's own pressure.
  A tagline built on table stakes rather than what the firm does uniquely is "merely serviceable"
  (DeSantis Breindel, "B2B Taglines: The Hardest Working Words in Branding", fetched 06.11.).
- In Germany, English claims are understood less than their senders assume: 64 percent of 1,204
  respondents said they do not understand English claims correctly, and only 48 percent said they
  know what German claims mean (YouGov Deutschland, 2015 fieldwork, published 25.02.2016,
  fetched 06.11.). The finding is ten years old and about consumer claims. It still argues for a
  German claim of its own rather than the English one on German pages.
- The searches turned up no controlled study of tagline length or of outcome against mood framing
  for B2B services. Practitioner advice favours short lines and one outcome. The scores below test
  that directly on h&w's own surfaces.

## Candidates

16 pairs in round 1. Ten are this run's own drafts. Three come from an independent draft: a Claude
model that saw the offer, the buyer and the rules, but neither the current claim nor d. It wrote six
pairs, and three were taken. Three anchors ran unmarked: the current claim, d, and e (d shortened).
The `voice` skill's rules applied to every draft: no hype, no contrast frame, no triplet, ihr-form
German that reads spoken.

## Method

- Medium and size: the website home hero at 1440 x 900, with the live page's lead sentence under the
  H1 and the hero visual frozen in one state (reduced motion), and the deck closing slide at
  1920 x 1080 from the slides catalog. Both were rendered headless from the live pages with the
  candidate set in place of the claim, then stacked into one image per pair and language, 842 x 1000.
  The closing slide's highlight word was off for every candidate, the current claim included.
- Personas: a CTO of a Mittelstand manufacturer under board pressure, a VP of Engineering at a
  scale-up, a Head of Engineering worried about quality and morale, a brand strategist for DACH B2B
  tech services, and a senior developer reading the closing slide after a two-day training.
- Criteria, fixed before the first score: clear, credible, distinctive, energy and calm, memorable,
  language (each 0 to 10), and an overall score from 0 to 100.
- Reviewers: Claude (`claude -p --model opus`) and Codex (`gpt-6.1-sol`, high). Each saw its own
  shuffled order under neutral labels, with nothing marking a line as current or naming its author.
- Round 1 ran twice per reviewer. Noise per reviewer is 4.7 points overall (criteria 0.4 to 0.6).
  On the panel mean, the number the tables compare, it is 1.3 points.
- Round 2 refined the top five on the reasons the reviewers gave: the short lines say nothing about
  agents, and the long ones are too long for a footer or signature. It ran once per reviewer, with
  the current claim and d as controls. The controls moved by 1.3 to 3.0 points between rounds.

Surfaces rendered: the home hero and the closing slide. Not rendered: the one-pager claim line, the
website footer, LinkedIn banners, the mail signature and the press boilerplate. The reviewers were
told that the line stands in each of these places.

## What keeps the top two below full marks

For a, "Calm teams ship sooner with agents.": four of five Codex reviewers read "calm" as a
precondition rather than an outcome ("Calm appears to describe teams already qualified to benefit").
All five Claude reviewers found the German word order "liefern mit Agenten früher" clunky. The fix
"Mit Agenten liefern gelassene Teams früher." has not been scored.

For b, "Ship sooner with agents, and stay steady.": the "and stay steady" tail read as an
afterthought, and the German "ruhig bleiben" as an instruction ("can sound like an instruction to
tolerate pressure rather than support for changing how we work").

For every candidate, distinctive stayed at or below 6.5: "sooner" is something any delivery firm can
say.
