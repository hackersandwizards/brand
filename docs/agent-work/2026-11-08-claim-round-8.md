# Claim round 8: Benedikt's line and fresh lines against the chosen f6, 08.11.2026

f6 stays: "Every agent needs a team in tune." / "Jeder KI-Agent braucht ein eingespieltes Team.",
with the German home H1 "zusammenspielen". No line of five rounds leads it outside the noise in
both runs and with both models. The runner-up is m3, "Agents write code. Your team ships
products." / "KI-Agenten schreiben Code. Euer Team liefert Produkte.": it leads f6 on the banners,
slide and link preview by 2.9 (6.0 with Codex) but trails by 0.2 with Claude, and ties it exactly as a
whole set with the site and the profile texts (76.5 each). So no decision item opens.

Benedikt's line, "Every agent needs your teams in tune and your company in flow.", fixes what f6
lacks alone on a banner: 32 of 32 readers read its teams as people (f6: 1 of 32). It scores 30.7
points below f6, though, in both runs and with both models: too long, two images stacked, and no
reader names a result for the company or its customers.

## Question

Benedikt decided f6 with "zusammenspielen" on 08.11. and proposed a line that would show the
people and the company again. He then asked for iteration until a final proposal, and later
described the thought the claim must carry: agents alone deliver nothing; agents and the human
teams in tune and in flow; then the company succeeds and ships features to its customers; a cool,
short, punchy line. His line went in as stated and in three variants, beside fresh lines on that
thought, f6 and n02 as unmarked anchors. Reviewers never learned which line is his or which is
chosen.

The rule, set before any score: f6 stays unless a variant leads outside the noise in both runs and
with both models, and reads as people in the cold read.

## Method

- Criteria, fixed before the first score: round 7's six (distinctive, clear, emotional, credible,
  calm, language), and two new ones, each 0 to 10: core ("a reader takes away that AI agents on
  their own deliver little, that they pay off only with the human teams working in tune with them,
  and that the company then delivers to its customers") and people ("it is clear that human people
  are meant, not more agents or software"). Overall 0 to 100.
- Rounds 1 to 3, text: CTO, VP of Engineering, conference organiser, brand strategist; Claude
  (`claude -p --model opus`) and Codex (`gpt-6.1-sol`, high); one run each in rounds 1 and 2, two
  in round 3 for noise. Each reviewer gave what keeps each line below the maximum; the next round
  fixed one or two causes. Beside them, cold reads of the line as text: "Next to a company logo
  stands only this line", CTO and HR at a logistics company, both models, English and German, 8
  per line, answered in free text ("what does this line claim? what kind of company would say
  it?").
- Rounds 4 and 5, rendered: the panel of round 7 (CTO, VP of Engineering, conference organiser,
  senior engineer looking for a job, brand strategist; both models; two runs; 20 runs per round),
  on the LinkedIn, X and Sessionize banners with the descriptor, the closing slide and the link
  preview, built as round 7 built them. New: the first banner is the LinkedIn profile as its narrow
  layout shows it, the real profile photo over the banner (the real screenshot of 31.10. in os
  `docs/agent-work/2026-10-31-channel-banners-live/`), so the overlap of `work-banner-photo-overlap`
  is in view.
- Cold reads of the LinkedIn banner alone, round 7's method: CTO, VP of Engineering, engineering
  lead, HR; both models; two runs with the answer options reversed; 16 per line and language. Two
  questions, each in its own run: the team question with "unclear" offered, and the free-text
  question above.
- The free-text readings went to a separate Claude run that saw only the readings, never the line,
  and marked each: people (agents need or work with human people), alone (agents alone are not
  enough), company (a result for the company or its customers: shipping, releases, features,
  products, success), and the kind of company. Core counts a reading with both people and company.
  Spot checks of 16 readings against their marks found none wrong.
- The set, f6, k1 and m3, round 7's method: every surface in place (LinkedIn and YouTube on the
  real screenshots, XING and Sessionize live on desktop and phone, the X header, slide, link
  preview, the AE-LEAD one-pager), with the home H1 (German "zusammenspielen" for every set), the
  lead, the tuning desk, the footer, the 24 decided page headlines (website item
  `work-ship-decided-page-headlines`, read from `origin/main`) and Benedikt's profile texts as
  round 7 read them that morning. CTO, VP of Engineering, brand strategist, bilingual copy editor;
  both models; two runs: 16 runs.
- Noise: the gap between the means of run 1 and run 2; for counts, the gap in a count. A lead
  counts only outside the noise, in both runs and with both models.
- 1,120 runs: 2 drafting, 472 text rounds (32 panel, 440 cold reads), 40 rendered panel, 576
  banner cold reads, 16 set, 14 classifier.
